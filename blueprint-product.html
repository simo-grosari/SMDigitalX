Section 2 · Printable reference

Storytelling Formula Cheat Sheet

The Hero's Journey, adapted for B2B services. Print this page and keep it by your desk; it is laminate ready.

1. Ordinary world

One sentence: what did the client's business or life look like before they found you?

2. The call

One sentence: what problem or moment pushed them to look for a solution?

3. The struggle

One sentence: what did they try that did not work, and why?

4. Meeting the guide

One sentence: how did they find you, and why did they trust you?

5. The plan

One sentence: what was the unique method or framework you gave them?

6. The transformation

One sentence: what changed in their business or life as a result?

7. The new normal

One sentence: what does success look like for them now?

8. The invitation

One sentence: what do you want the reader to do next?

Section 4 · Editable

Sales Page Storytelling Script

Work through each of the five sections. Copywriting prompts are included under each box to guide what you write.

One or two lines that stop the scroll. What is the single most surprising or relatable thing about your client's situation?
Name the problem in the words your client would use, not industry jargon.
Tell one real or composite client story: before, the turning point, and the result.
Name your method. Give it 3 to 5 steps the reader can picture themselves following.
One or two client results, testimonials, or numbers that back up the transformation story.
Tell the reader exactly what to do next, and what happens right after they do it.

Section 5 · Printable examples

Industry Specific Customisation Examples

Three worked examples showing how the storytelling framework changes across different service businesses.

Virtual Assistant

A virtual assistant serving overwhelmed small business owners repositioned her messaging from a list of tasks to a specific transformation.

Before: "I offer admin support, email management, and calendar scheduling."
After: "I help small business owners get their evenings back by running their inbox and calendar like clockwork, so meetings stop colliding and emails stop piling up."
Executive Coach

An executive coach moved from generic leadership language to a story built around a single recognisable turning point.

Before: "I coach leaders to reach their full potential."
After: "I work with newly promoted managers in their first 90 days, when the team still sees them as a peer, so they can lead with authority without losing the relationships that got them there."
Brand Designer

A brand designer replaced a portfolio led pitch with a story about the business outcome her clients actually wanted.

Before: "I design logos, websites, and brand guidelines."
After: "I help founders who are embarrassed to send people to their own website turn a DIY looking brand into one that makes people trust them before the first call."

Resources & Next Steps

  • Copywriting tools: Hemingway Editor (free, checks readability), Grammarly (free tier, catches grammar slips)
  • Video editing, free: CapCut, DaVinci Resolve
  • Video editing, paid: Adobe Premiere Rush, Descript
  • Legal document resources: A solicitor or a template library such as Rocket Lawyer or Termly for terms and privacy pages
  • Recommended reading: "Building a StoryBrand" by Donald Miller, "Made to Stick" by Chip and Dan Heath, "This Is Marketing" by Seth Godin
  • Design tools: Canva for social graphics and sales page mockups, Google Sheets for tracking your client pipeline
